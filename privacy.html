Sparely Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Sparely does not collect your data. There is no Sparely server, no account to create, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party tracking of any kind. Everything you record stays on your device and in your own private cloud storage: iCloud on iPhone and iPad, and your Google account's backup on Android.

What Sparely stores, and where

Sparely is a personal-finance ledger. The entries, amounts, accounts, labels, templates and preferences you create are written to a database on your device.

On iPhone and iPad, if you are signed in to iCloud and iCloud Drive is enabled, that database is also synced to the private CloudKit database of your own Apple Account, so it is backed up and available on your other devices.

That iCloud copy belongs to you. It sits in your Apple Account, under Apple's terms and Apple's encryption, in transit and at rest. The developer of Sparely has no access to it and no way to read it. Apple does not give the developer your ledger, your identity or your usage.

On Android there is no sync. If backup is turned on for your device, Android's Auto Backup saves a copy of that database and your preferences to your own Google account, under Google's terms and encryption, and can restore it when you set up a new device. That copy belongs to you. The developer of Sparely has no access to it and no way to read it. You can turn it off in your device's backup settings.

Your App Lock PIN is never included in an Android backup. It is stored encrypted on your device with a key that stays there, so App Lock starts switched off after a restore.

Data the developer collects: none

Sparely has no back-end. There is no server operated by the developer that your data is sent to, and no place where it could be stored, sold or shared. Concretely, Sparely contains:

  • no analytics or product-measurement SDK;
  • no crash-reporting or diagnostics SDK;
  • no advertising SDK, ad identifiers and no advertising of any kind;
  • no third-party tracking, and no tracking across apps or websites owned by other companies, so on iPhone and iPad Sparely never presents an App Tracking Transparency prompt;
  • no third-party dependencies at all on iPhone and iPad, and on Android only the standard Android and Kotlin libraries.

This is why Sparely's App Store privacy label is Data Not Collected, with Tracking: No, and why its Google Play Data safety section says no data is collected or shared. No financial information, identifiers, contacts, usage data or diagnostics are collected, and none are linked to you.

The one network request Sparely makes

Sparely fetches a table of daily exchange rates so it can convert between currencies. It asks ExchangeRate-API (open.er-api.com) and, if that is unavailable, Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev), which republishes the European Central Bank's daily reference rates.

These requests use HTTPS and carry no personal data: they ask for the public rate table for a base currency and nothing else. No entry, amount, account name, identifier or device identifier is sent. As with any request to any website, the provider necessarily sees the network address it came from; neither provider is used for tracking, and neither receives anything that identifies you or your ledger. The rates are cached on device, so the request happens at most once a day.

Purchases

Sparely is free on iPhone, iPad and Android. It has no purchases or subscriptions, so it never handles payment details.

When you contact the developer

Send Feedback opens an email for you to read and send, so nothing leaves your device unless you send it. On iPhone and iPad it opens in Mail or your mail app; with no mail account set up, the message is copied for you to paste into an email instead. On Android it opens in your own email app. The developer then receives your email address and what you wrote. A bug report also lists the app version, iOS or Android version, device model, language and region, main currency, number of entries, storage status and short summaries of recent crashes, and on iPhone and iPad your iCloud account status. It adds no amounts, notes or account names.

For messages you send, the data controller is Borislav Borisov, the developer of Sparely, based in Bulgaria. The legal basis is legitimate interest: messages are used only to reply to you and to fix or improve Sparely, and are kept only as long as that takes. You can ask to see or delete them at the address below, and you can complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Sharing with other people

On iPhone and iPad, Sparely can let you share an account with someone else using Apple's CloudKit sharing — for example, a household budget shared with a partner. Sharing only ever happens because you started it, by sending an invitation from inside the app. Sparely on Android has no account sharing.

When you share, the person who accepts the invitation can see the shared account and the entries in it, including their amounts, dates, categories, labels and notes, and — depending on the permission you grant — can add, edit or delete entries in it. They cannot see anything you have not shared: your other accounts, your other entries and your preferences remain private to you.

The share is carried by Apple's CloudKit. The developer is not a participant and cannot read it. If you stop sharing, or remove a participant, CloudKit withdraws their access and the shared records are removed from their devices; copies they made outside Sparely, such as an export, are of course beyond the app's reach. If someone shares an account with you, that data belongs to them, and they can revoke your access at any time in the same way.

Deleting everything

You can delete all of your data from inside the app: Settings → Manage Data → Delete all data. It permanently deletes the entries, accounts, labels and templates you own, from this device and from your iCloud copy, and resets your preferences. It cannot be undone. Data owned by someone else and shared with you is not deleted by this — only its owner can delete it — but you can leave a share you were invited to.

Deleting the app itself removes the on-device copy. To remove the iCloud copy as well, use Delete all data before deleting the app, or remove Sparely's iCloud data in the Settings app under your Apple Account.

On Android, Delete all data clears your device, and the next backup replaces your saved copy. Deleting the app removes the on-device copy but not a backup already in your Google account. You can delete device backups in Google Drive.

Children

Sparely is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone, of any age.
